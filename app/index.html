ℹ️ About This Tool

Overview

This platform is an interactive decision-support tool for exploring the techno-economic potential of solar-powered irrigation across 40 Sub-Saharan African countries. It visualizes over 132,000 rainfed cropland grid cells (10 km × 10 km resolution) with crop-specific metrics spanning irrigation demand, energy requirements, economic viability, and environmental parameters.

The tool is designed for policymakers, development practitioners, and researchers to identify high-potential regions for solar irrigation investment at both national and sub-national levels.

Map Metrics Explained

MetricUnitWhat It Measures
🌾 Yield Gainton/haThe additional crop yield obtained from irrigation compared to rainfed-only production. Higher values indicate locations where irrigation has the greatest agronomic impact.
💧 Water Requirementm³/ha/yrThe volume of irrigation water needed per hectare per year to supplement rainfall. Calculated as the gap between crop water demand and effective rainfall.
⚡ Energy RequirementkWh/ha/yrThe electrical energy needed to pump groundwater for irrigation, accounting for depth-to-water, pump efficiency, and water volume requirements.
💰 Break-even PriceUSD/tonThe minimum crop selling price at which solar irrigation investment becomes financially viable. Accounts for PV system cost, borehole drilling, pump, and installation. Lower is better.
☀ Solar RadiationkWh/m²/dayAverage daily solar irradiance at the surface. Determines how much energy a photovoltaic panel can generate at each location.
🕳 Groundwater DepthmetersEstimated depth to the water table. Deeper water tables require more energy to pump and increase borehole drilling costs.
🔋 PV System SizekWp/haThe peak capacity of the solar photovoltaic system required to power irrigation for one hectare of cropland.
💦 Groundwater YieldL/sEstimated sustainable borehole abstraction rate. Low yields may limit the feasibility of groundwater-based irrigation.

Fertilizer Scenarios

The platform models four fertilizer application scenarios to reflect the range of farming practices across SSA:

ScenarioMeaning
FERT100100% of the agronomically optimal fertilizer rate. Represents the theoretical maximum yield with full nutrient supply.
FERT8585% of optimal. A minor reduction simulating slight resource constraints or sub-optimal timing.
FERT5050% of optimal. Represents moderately resource-constrained smallholder farmers.
FERT2525% of optimal. Represents severely constrained conditions with minimal fertilizer access.

Lower fertilizer rates generally result in lower yield gains from irrigation and higher break-even prices, reflecting the interaction between water and nutrient availability.

Methodology

Grid Resolution

All simulations are performed on a 10 km × 10 km grid (approximately 0.0833° at the equator). Each cell represents a spatial unit for which crop growth, water balance, and economic calculations are performed.

Temporal Coverage

Simulations span 19 years (2001–2019). For each cell, the platform reports the temporal mean alongside standard deviation, minimum, and maximum values to capture inter-annual climate variability.

Economic Viability

A cell is classified as economically viable when its break-even crop price falls below the current market price. The break-even calculation considers:

  • Capital cost of the PV system (panels, inverter, mounting)
  • Borehole drilling cost (proportional to depth-to-water)
  • Pump and irrigation equipment costs
  • System lifetime and discount rate
  • Annual yield gain from irrigation (the revenue side)

Cropland Filtering

Only grid cells with existing rainfed cropland (as identified by SPAM2017) are included. This restricts the analysis to areas where farming already takes place, rather than proposing irrigation on uncultivated land.

Road Distance

For Rwanda and Kenya, the platform calculates the distance from each cell's centroid (center point) to the nearest paved road segment. Road classifications (paved vs. unpaved) are derived from machine learning models applied to Sentinel-2 satellite imagery.

This metric serves as a proxy for market access and logistics feasibility — cells closer to paved roads face lower transport costs for both agricultural inputs and harvested produce.

Note: Road data is currently available for Rwanda and Kenya only. Continental coverage is planned pending additional ML-predicted road datasets.

Administrative Boundaries & Choropleth Mode

Toggling "Show boundaries" activates choropleth mode, which:

  1. Loads the ADM1 (first-level administrative) boundary polygons for the selected country from geoBoundaries.
  2. Groups all grid cells within each administrative region using a pre-computed point-in-polygon spatial lookup (132,083 cells mapped across 486 regions).
  3. Calculates the average metric value across all cells in each region.
  4. Colors each region on a green-to-red gradient (7-stop color ramp), with metric-appropriate inversion (e.g., green = high yield gain, but green = low break-even price).

This enables policy-level analysis at the provincial/county scale, as requested by decision-makers who operate at the sub-national administrative level.

Investment Analysis Filters

The filter panel allows setting minimum and/or maximum thresholds on any combination of metrics simultaneously. Only cells matching ALL active filters are displayed on the map. This enables targeted site identification, for example:

  • High-potential sites: Yield Gain > 3 t/ha AND Break-even < $200/ton
  • Accessible sites: Road Distance < 10 km AND GW Depth < 30 m
  • Water-efficient sites: Water Req. < 2000 m³/ha AND Cropland > 30%

The filter match counter at the bottom shows how many cells satisfy the active criteria out of the total dataset.
